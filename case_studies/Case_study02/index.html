SETTING THE SCENE

The puzzle screen is where players spend most of their time.

DESIGN BRIEF

'How can we enhance the clarity, consistency & usability of key interactive elements on the game board, ensuring players can understand & engage effortlessly? Envision a UI that guides naturally through the game journey.'

PROBLEM SPACE

Every friction point on the puzzle screen multiplies

In order to know the existing friction points on the screen & area of opportunities which can help to enhance the gameplay experience, the puzzle needed a systematic UX audit.

01

Readability & Tap Accuracy

Tiles were small, causing mistaps — especially for older players. The rack was undersized, making the “snap into place” feel weak. Multiple daily support tickets requesting bigger tiles.

Small tiles Mistaps Accessibility
02

Visual Hierarchy Breakdown

Power-ups blended with the tiles (both square). The rack lacked distinction from the board. Jewel notifs were oversized, dominating the icon space.

No differentiation Unclear hierarchy Visual noise
03

Lack of Gameplay Feedback

No gratification on making matches. No rack fill feedback. Silent interactions made the game feel unresponsive. Players had no sense on momentum or progress during active gameplay.

No feedback Silent UI Low engagement
04

UI Inconsistency

Extra slot looked nothing like other powerups despite functioning as one. The w2e icon was visually inconsistent with the home screen version. Too many similar tones of same colour family.

Inconsistent elements Visual clutter Disjointed palette

Research & UX Reasoning

Every change backed by a design principle

I conducted competitive analysis across 6 scaling tile-match games — Tile Explorer, Tile Club, Tile Family, Triple Tile, Match Factory, and Dream Blast — to benchmark UI patterns and validate design decisions.

Key findings from competitive analysis

All competitors use circular power-up holders to differentiate from square tiles

Competitors have neutral-toned power-ups keeping screen visually light

Very small jewel notifs positioned at bottom-right, secondary to the power-up icon itself

Extra slot UI is visually similar to other power-ups (law of uniform connectedness)

Competitor screens are optimised for power-up discovery & usage

Prominent rack visibility with rack holders or distinct borders for clear tile distinction

Fitts's Law

Bigger tiles = fewer mistaps. Power-ups closer to the board = improved discoverability & usage. Increased tap target sizes across all interactive elements.

Applied to: Tile size, power-up size & positioning

Jakob's Law

Players are playing other tile-match games with bigger tiles and find those games easier to play. Our UI should match the mental models players already have.

Applied to: Tile size, overall layout

Recognition over Recall

The w2e icon on the puzzle screen was visually different from the homescreen version. Players shouldn't have to remember what an icon does — it should be instantly recognisable.

Applied to: W2E icon redesign

Law of Uniform Connectedness

The Extra Slot functionally acts as a power-up, but its visual treatment was completely different. Elements that share a function should share a visual language.

Applied to: Extra slot, power-up consistency

Gestalt — Similarity

Almost all elements on the board were squarish — tiles and power-ups were indistinguishable. Circular power-ups vs. square tiles creates clear visual grouping.

Applied to: Circular power-up holders

Feedback Heuristic (Nielsen)

Every action deserves a reaction. Matches had no acknowledgement, rack fills were silent. Adding gratification text and rack fill feedback creates a sense of momentum.

Applied to: Match gratification, rack fill feedback

The Change & UX Reasoning

Every change backed by design principles

A

Tile & Rack System

Making the core gameplay elements bigger, clearer, and more intuitive.

Bigger Tile Size

Shifted from square to slightly rectangular tiles to increase tile size within the existing board constraints, without altering the grid structure.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Larger tiles → easier to scan, recognise, & decide which match to make.
  • Reduce → mistap, especially for older age group players.
  • Jakob’s Law → players are playing other games with bigger tile sizes.
  • Fewer mistaps → less frustration, which can positively impact session length and return rate.

Bigger Rack Size + Tint Behind Rack

Increased the rack size to enhance the feel of the tiles “snapping” into place — simulating real world interactions. Added a tint behind the rack for visual distinction of empty slots.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Stronger feedback loop → larger rack enhances the perceived “snap,” making interactions feel more satisfying and responsive.
  • Improved visibility → Background tint helps players instantly identify available slots, reducing cognitive load.
  • Real-world affordance → Mimics the tactile behaviour of physical tile games, making interactions feel more intuitive.

After image

Rack Moved Above the Tile Set

Repositioned the rack from below the board to above it, establishing a natural top-down visual hierarchy: Rack → Board → Controls.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Rack at top becomes the first focal point, encouraging more calculative decisions before making a match.
  • Give a clear visual hierarchy: “what I have → what actions I can take.”
  • Aligns with natural eye movement (top-down scanning), creating a smoother, more intuitive gameplay flow.

After image

Rack Holder Introduced

When tiles were placed on the rack, they fully covered the slot indicators, blurring the visual distinction between rack tiles and board tiles, especially in levels with greater vertical height and higher tile density.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Creates a sense of depth and containment, simulating the real-world feel of placing tiles into a physical tray rather than floating them on a flat surface.
  • Scales across complexity → Remains effective even in levels with higher vertical density.

B

Power-Up Redesign

Creating clear visual distinction between interactive and passive game elements.

Circular Power-Up Holders

Replaced squarish power-up holder with circular ones. Updated the colour scheme to align with the current game UI. Moved power-ups slightly higher for better anchoring.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Stronger visual differentiation → Circular shape clearly distinguishes power-ups from tile elements.
  • Faster recognition → Unique form factor enables instant identification during gameplay.
  • Improved spatial anchoring → Higher placement makes power-ups feel as persistent UI elements rather than board content, reinforcing their role as player tools.

After image

Extra Slot Aligned with Power-Ups

The extra slot functionally acts as a power-up, yet its visual treatment was entirely inconsistent — a distinct purple-gold palette that felt disconnected from the rest of the UI.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Law of Uniform Connectedness → elements sharing a function should share visual language.
  • Reduced visual noise → Eliminates colour clashes with existing UI elements.
  • Better discoverability → Consistent styling helps players quickly identify it as part of the power-up system.

After image

Jewel Notification Refinement

Compared to competitor games, the jewel notification badges on our power-ups were noticeably larger and dominated the icon space. The badge is a secondary signal; it should inform, not overpower.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Improved visual hierarchy → Smaller badge size ensures the power-up remains the primary focus.
  • Better visibility → Red colour universally recognised as a notification indicator across platforms.
  • Clearer system states → Enhanced disabled (no-internet) state with improved iconography to communicate unavailability without ambiguity.
Before image
After image

Neutral-Toned & Bigger Power-Ups

Updated power-ups to neutral tones (on the game board) to differentiate them from multi-coloured tiles. Also increased power up size & reduced gap between power-ups & tiles.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Stronger visual separation → Neutral tones prevent competition with vibrant tiles, giving power-ups a distinct visual lane.
  • Improved discoverability → Increased size improves tap accuracy and makes power-ups feel like intentional, prominent tools.
  • Fitt’s law — Reducing the gap between power-ups and tiles shortens the interaction distance, making power-ups feel naturally connected to the board.

Aligning the watch-to-earn icon with the rest of the UI for consistency and better conversions.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Improved consistency → Aligning with the home screen icon strengthens recognition and reduces cognitive friction.
  • Higher conversion cues → Replacing “+60 coins” with “Free” leverages stronger motivational triggers.
  • Reduced visual noise → Fewer colour variations create a more balanced and less overwhelming toolbar.

D

Gameplay Feedback

Turning a silent game board into a responsive, rewarding experience.

Match Gratification & Rack Fill Feedback

Introduced celebratory text feedback (e.g., “On a Roll”, “Awesome”, “Amazing”) on successful matches and visual feedback when player reaches rack fill state for awareness. The board was repositioned slightly downward to accommodate these animations without UI overlap.

WHY THIS WORKS
  • Immediate system feedback → Aligns with Nielsen’s Feedback Heuristic, reinforcing that every action is acknowledged.
  • Emotional design layer → these moments of micro-delight & awareness elevate the experience beyond pure puzzle mechanics, contributing to the overall personality and tone of the game.
  • Clear feedback visibility → Dedicated space ensures animations remain noticeable and unobstructed.

A/B testing & Impact

The numbers that validate the design decisions

Experimentation Framework

Phased Rollout

Each change was shipped individually, one element at a time, so the data analyst could clearly attribute metric movements to specific changes.

A/B Split

Each change was tested with a control vs. variant split across a statistically significant user base before full rollout.

Data — Informed iteration

Insights from performance data and player feedback were continuously analysed not just to validate decisions but also to guide further refinements and optimizations.

REFLECTION

Learnings from the Project

01

Small, principled changes compound

No single change drives impact in isolation. Consistent improvements across usability, clarity, and feedback can collectively lead to meaningful gains in engagement. Well-designed systems outperform isolated optimizations.

02

Ship one change at a time

Shipping changes individually allowed us to measure the impact of each change in isolation. Measuring one variable at a time ensures reliable insights and reduces ambiguity in decision-making.

03

Sometimes constraints drive better solutions

Design limitations often lead to more thoughtful and efficient solutions. Working within constraints encourages prioritization and leads to outcomes that are both practical and scalable.

04

Validate assumptions with data

Intuitive design decisions don’t always translate to better outcomes. Continuous testing and validation are critical to ensure that solutions align with actual user behaviour.

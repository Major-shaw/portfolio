Setting the scene

We saw a clear drop in player retention after Day 1. A lot of players were not returning the next day (Rolling Retention).

To understand WHY, we analysed a week of player behaviour data and reviewed 7–8 play test sessions, allowing us to align ultimate goal and design decisions with real player needs.

The Problem

This chart shows engagement and retention patterns over one week for players who installed the game, highlighting how activity changes as players return across multiple days.

💡 Key Insights

01

A large portion of week-2 players lack a reason to come back daily.

02

Engagement compounds with each additional return.

🧠 What we analysed

Need an appointment mechanic that gives players a clear, low-effort reason to return every day, without increasing cognitive or gameplay complexity.

Focus should be on repeat activation, ensuring visible progress and rewards within first few returns to reinforce the habit loop.

System need to scale across player types, supporting quick, casual sessions while also allowing highly engaged players to progress without artificial caps.

Hypothesis

The game lacks a clear system to encourage daily returns. This might be due to limited daily motivation, lack of clear progression goals, repetitive content or external factors like time constraints.

Improving perceived purpose in daily sessions should lead to stronger multi-day retention and engagement.

Brainstorming

Insights & Next Steps

After launch, data surfaced two clear signals that shaped the next iteration.

Highest Intent

Taps on the Quest event icon were the highest among all event icons on the Home Screen.

Highest Claim Rate

Reward claim rate for completed quests was the highest compared to all other in-game events.

Players who discovered the feature engaged deeply. The challenge shifted from retention to discoverability.

The Opportunity

Despite strong intent signals, a significant portion of players never reached the feature — because they never encountered it. Players spend the majority of session time on the game board, completing quests passively without awareness. The value was invisible to them.

Optimisation

01

Intro Overlay

A lightweight overlay surfaced on the game board during the player's first session of the day. Designed to increase awareness without interrupting the core play loop.

Surfaces once per session.

02

Quest Icon on Game Board

A persistent Quest icon placed directly on the game board. Reduces dependency on Home Screen navigation and brings the feature to where players spend the most time.

Meets players where they actually play.
